# Consulting Hours & Capacity Planning — Draft

## 1. Hours-per-deliverable benchmarks (starting estimates, log your actuals and replace these)

| Deliverable | Estimated hours | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Formula screening (Annex II–VI check) | 1–3h | Faster with a templated checklist; longer for complex/multi-active formulas |
| CPSR Part A drafting | 4–10h | Depends on how complete the client's raw data is (physico-chem, micro, exposure) |
| Coordinating Safety Assessor sign-off (Part B) | 1–2h | Your time is liaison/packaging, not the assessment itself |
| Label review & compliance check | 1–3h | Per SKU/variant; more if multi-language |
| Claims substantiation review | 2–6h | Scales with number and risk-level of claims |
| CPNP notification (data ready) | 1–2h | Mechanical once inputs are complete |
| Full PIF assembly (single SKU) | 10–20h | Sum of the above plus QC/assembly time |
| UK SCPN notification + RP coordination (add-on) | 3–6h | Separate legal process, don't underestimate as "just another portal" |
| Ongoing retainer client, steady state | 3–8h/month | Ad hoc questions, formula/label change reviews, Annex-update monitoring |

Track actuals per engagement from day one — these ranges should converge
quickly to your real numbers, which is what makes fixed-fee pricing safe.

## 2. Solo consultant capacity planning

- A 40h/week solo practice does **not** mean 40 billable hours. Realistic
  **utilization rate** (billable ÷ total working hours) for a solo consultant:
  - Year 1 (building pipeline): ~40–55%
  - Steady state, established practice: ~60–70%
  - The rest goes to business development, admin/invoicing, CPD (staying
    current on Annex amendments, SCCS opinions, EU guidance), and buffer.
- At 55% utilization on a 40h week, that's ~22 billable hours/week —
  useful as your denominator when sanity-checking whether a retainer or
  package load is actually sustainable alongside new client acquisition.
- Build in explicit **non-billable regulatory monitoring time** (tracking
  Annex II–VI amendments, new SCCS opinions, Commission guidance) — this is
  real, recurring work that protects your fixed-fee margins and should be
  budgeted as overhead, not squeezed out.

## 3. Scoping new engagements

- Before quoting a fixed fee, walk the client through the
  `regulatory/market-entry-checklist.md` phases to identify which are already
  done (existing CPSR data, prior claims testing, etc.) vs. need to start from
  scratch — this is the single biggest driver of actual hours vs. estimate.
- For multi-SKU engagements, don't assume linear scaling — the first SKU in a
  new product line carries most of the formula-screening/process-setup
  overhead; subsequent similar SKUs (same brand, similar formula family) are
  typically 30–50% faster.
- Pad new/unfamiliar work types (a novel ingredient, a first-time market like
  UK or CH, a first-time client industry) with a contingency buffer (~20%)
  until you have actuals to price against.

## 4. Retainer sizing conversation with clients

Frame retainer tiers around what the client actually needs steady-state, not
a round number:
- **Light (5h/month):** single mature product line, infrequent formula/label
  changes, mostly "on-call" reassurance.
- **Standard (10h/month):** active product line(s) with periodic changes,
  claims reviews, or a client team that asks questions regularly.
- **Active (20h/month):** multiple SKUs in flux, frequent NPD, or a client
  without in-house regulatory capability leaning on you as their de facto RA
  function.

If a retainer client consistently overruns their bucket, that's a signal to
either move them up a tier or price the overage hours explicitly (e.g., 1.25x
the retainer's effective hourly rate) — decide the policy up front and put it
in the engagement letter rather than negotiating it after the fact.
