# EU Cosmetics Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 — Working Reference

Status: working reference for internal use. Annexes (II–VI) are amended
frequently by Commission Regulations — always verify a specific ingredient's
current status against the consolidated text on EUR-Lex before it goes into a
client deliverable. This doc is the "what to check and where," not a substitute
for the consolidated text itself.

## 1. Scope

Applies to any "cosmetic product" (Art. 2) made available on the market in the
EU/EEA (EU27 + Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein), intended for external contact
with skin, hair, nails, lips, external genital organs, teeth, or mucous
membranes of the oral cavity, for cleansing, perfuming, changing appearance,
protecting, keeping in good condition, or correcting body odours.

Borderline calls (cosmetic vs. biocide vs. medical device vs. medicinal
product) are one of the most common early questions in a new client engagement
— worth a dedicated qualification check before anything else, since it
determines which regulation (1223/2009 vs. Biocidal Products Regulation
528/2012 vs. MDR vs. medicinal product rules) governs at all.

## 2. Responsible Person (RP) — Art. 4–5

- A product cannot be placed on the EU/EEA market without a designated RP
  established within the EU/EEA.
- RP can be the manufacturer, importer, distributor (if repackaging/rebranding),
  or a **third party appointed by mandate** — this is a common consulting
  service offering (acting as, or arranging, RP).
- RP duties: ensure compliance (safety, labelling, claims), keep the PIF
  accessible, cooperate with competent authorities, notify serious undesirable
  effects, withdraw/recall non-compliant products.
- Non-EU/EEA brands entering the EU market **must** have an EU-established RP
  — this is a recurring, concrete need for consulting clients launching from
  outside the EU (UK post-Brexit, US, etc.).

## 3. Product Information File (PIF) — Art. 11

Must be kept at the RP's address, accessible to authorities for **10 years**
after the last batch is placed on the market. Contains:

1. Product description
2. Cosmetic Product Safety Report (CPSR — see below)
3. Description of manufacturing method + GMP compliance statement (ISO 22716
   is the standard reference for demonstrating GMP)
4. Proof of claimed effect, where justified by the nature of the claim
5. Data on any animal testing performed (by manufacturer, agents, suppliers) —
   relevant to compliance with the animal testing ban, Art. 18

## 4. Cosmetic Product Safety Report (CPSR) — Annex I

Two parts:
- **Part A — Safety information**: qualitative/quantitative formula,
  physico-chemical characteristics, microbiological quality, impurities/traces,
  packaging material interaction, normal & foreseeable use, exposure, toxicological
  profile of substances, undesirable effects data, other relevant info.
- **Part B — Safety assessment**: assessment conclusion, warnings/instructions,
  reasoning, assessor's credentials and approval. **Must be signed off by a
  qualified Safety Assessor** (Art. 10) — this is a formal, non-delegable
  determination. A consulting practice can compile Part A and coordinate the
  process, but Part B sign-off needs a qualified assessor of record.

## 5. Notification — CPNP (Art. 13)

Before placing a product on the market, the RP must notify via the **Cosmetic
Products Notification Portal (CPNP)**: product category, name, RP contact,
country of origin/manufacture, presence of nanomaterials or CMR substances,
frame formulation, original labelling/photo. Notification is free but must be
done per product (per shade/variant in some cases) before sale.

## 6. Labelling — Art. 19

Required on container + packaging (or on an accompanying leaflet/tag/label
where impractical to fit on the product):

- Name/registered trade name and address of the RP
- Nominal content at time of packaging
- Date of minimum durability ("best used before the end of" symbol) OR
  Period-After-Opening (PAO) symbol (open jar icon + months) if shelf life >30
  months
- Particular precautions for use (including those in Annex III restrictions)
- Batch number or reference for identification
- Product function, unless obvious from presentation
- Ingredient list, preceded by "Ingredients"/"Composition", in **INCI**
  nomenclature, descending order of weight (below 1% may be listed in any order),
  colorants last (by CI number), allergens per Annex III/Regulation updates
  called out if present above threshold (0.001% leave-on / 0.01% rinse-off)
- Country of origin, for imported products
- Language: official language(s) of the Member State where made available

## 7. Restricted/Prohibited Substances — Annexes II–VI

- **Annex II** — prohibited substances (cannot be used in cosmetics at all)
- **Annex III** — substances subject to restrictions (max concentration, area
  of application, other conditions)
- **Annex IV** — permitted colorants (with conditions)
- **Annex V** — permitted preservatives (with conditions, concentration limits)
- **Annex VI** — permitted UV filters (with conditions, concentration limits)

CMR substances (carcinogenic, mutagenic, reprotoxic, categories 1A/1B/2) are
banned by default under Art. 15 unless a specific exemption is granted via the
SCCS opinion process.

**Practical note:** formula screening against current Annex II–VI status is one
of the highest-value, most repeatable consulting deliverables — worth
templating as a standard checklist per new client formula.

## 8. Claims — Regulation (EU) No 655/2013

Six common criteria all cosmetic claims must meet: legal compliance,
truthfulness, evidential support, honesty, fairness, informed decision-making.
Claims substantiation (efficacy testing, consumer perception studies) should be
retained in/referenced by the PIF. The EU also maintains guidance documents on
specific claim types (e.g., "free-from" claims, naturalness claims) worth
checking against for higher-risk claims.

For UK-facing claims specifically, this isn't the whole picture — the ad also
has to clear the CAP Code / ASA bar, which is a separate enforcement layer
with its own evidence expectations. See `uk-claims-asa-cap-code.md`.

## 9. Nanomaterials — Art. 16

Separate notification requirement 6 months before market placement if the
product contains nanomaterials, unless already covered by a colorant/UV
filter/preservative-specific Annex entry with defined nano conditions.

## 10. Post-market obligations

- **Serious undesirable effects (SUE)** — Art. 23: RP and distributors must
  notify the competent authority of the Member State where the SUE occurred.
- **Market surveillance / non-compliance** — competent authorities can request
  the PIF, require corrective action, or order withdrawal/recall.

## 11. Beyond the EU27 — other EMEA markets (do not assume EU rules transfer)

- **United Kingdom** — retained a near-identical framework post-Brexit (UK
  Cosmetics Regulation) but as a **separate legal regime**: separate
  notification via **SCPN (Submit Cosmetic Product Notification)** through the
  OPSS portal (not CPNP), a **UK-established Responsible Person** required
  (distinct from the EU RP — a GB/NI product needs its own), and Great Britain
  vs. Northern Ireland have different rules (NI stays aligned with EU 1223/2009
  under the Windsor Framework). A client selling in both the EU and UK needs
  **two RPs and two notifications**, not one.
- **Switzerland** — Cosmetic Ordinance (KOsV / OCos), largely harmonised with
  EU 1223/2009 in substance but with its own notification process (via the FOPH
  / Notify portal) and a Swiss-established responsible person requirement.
- **GCC / Middle East** — different framework entirely (GSO standards, national
  registration requirements e.g. Saudi SFDA, UAE); do not extrapolate from EU
  compliance — flag as a separate workstream if a client asks about MENA
  expansion.
- **Rule of thumb:** treat "EMEA" as a shorthand for "ask which specific
  jurisdiction," not a single ruleset. Default assumption in this repo is EU27
  unless the client/product is specified otherwise.

## 12. Medical devices (MDR) — borderline-product pointer, not a full reference

This repo is cosmetics-first; MDR (Regulation (EU) 2017/745) content here is
intentionally light since it's occasional crossover work, not the core
practice. Treat this as a flag-it-early pointer, not a substitute for
proper MDR-specific reference material if a device engagement gets deeper.

- **The classification question comes first, same instinct as Art. 2
  cosmetic qualification** (§1 above): does the product have a medical
  purpose (diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, treatment of disease/injury)
  via a mechanism that isn't primarily pharmacological/immunological/
  metabolic? If yes, it's a device, not a cosmetic — different regulation,
  different Responsible Person concept (MDR uses "Authorised
  Representative," not the cosmetics RP), different notification route
  (device registration via Eudamed / national competent authority, not
  CPNP), and a much heavier conformity route (risk classification I/IIa/
  IIb/III, Notified Body involvement for anything above Class I).
- **Common borderline territory from the cosmetics side**: contact lenses/
  lens care products, certain skin-adjacent devices (microneedling/derma-
  rollers depending on claims and mechanism), some UV-exposure or light-
  based devices, and products where the claimed *mechanism* (not just the
  claimed *effect*) crosses into physical/mechanical action rather than
  cosmetic function. The claim wording itself can be what tips
  classification — same trap as the medicinal-claim issue on the
  advertising side (see `uk-claims-asa-cap-code.md` §2).
- **Practical move**: flag classification as its own scoped question at
  intake for anything device-adjacent, before quoting cosmetics-style PIF/
  CPSR work — MDR conformity is a materially different (and heavier)
  engagement, not an add-on to a cosmetics package.
